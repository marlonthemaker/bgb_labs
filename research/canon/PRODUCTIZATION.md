# Product Engineering

Engineering exists to make the native-adoption research and applied-delivery loop more reproducible, trustworthy, and efficient.

Software does not create product maturity by itself. Product maturity comes from evidence, repeatability, claim discipline, and demonstrated user or customer value.

## Current Rule

Do not build generalized platform machinery merely because Hotel Shoreline has
demonstrated the controlled method. Product infrastructure requires repeated
research or customer use beyond one fictional application.

Deferred until earned:

- generic EvaluationWorld or benchmark frameworks;
- broad registries or intelligence graphs;
- HTTP APIs;
- databases;
- dashboards;
- authentication;
- billing;
- customer integrations;
- broad adapter architecture;
- public leaderboard;
- hosted evaluator or workflow systems.

## Allowed In Controlled And Pre-Pilot Work

- static fixture data;
- deterministic tool contracts;
- small schemas owned by the immediate research artifact;
- reproducible local runner when required by the cases;
- simple validation and analysis scripts;
- reviewed templates and reports.

A controlled or pre-pilot implementation should be easy to inspect, rerun, and
remove. It should not imply a future service boundary without evidence.

## Product Capability Maturity

Use these states for a method, treatment, tool, or offer. They are distinct from roadmap phases and evidence levels.

| State | Required basis | Allowed use |
|---|---|---|
| `concept` | Defined problem, target user, hypothesis, scope, and known risk | Internal discussion and experiment planning |
| `research_backed` | Scoped finding linked to controlled evidence and limitations | Follow-up research and treatment design |
| `treatment_backed` | Matched baseline and retest show a useful effect with regression review | Narrow internal or cooperative implementation |
| `trust_reviewed` | Evidence, evaluator, privacy, representation, and claim review complete | Controlled demo or design-partner discussion |
| `pilot_ready` | Repeatable inputs, outputs, delivery process, ownership, and failure handling | Narrow customer pilot within validated scope |
| `package_ready` | Versioned, supportable, repeatable delivery with approved claims and known economics | Packaged service or product capability |

`retired` may be used when a capability is no longer offered or valid.

Hotel Shoreline is a controlled method and engineering proof. Completing it
does not automatically make the method, intervention, or Native Adoption
Analysis pilot-ready.

## Readiness Rules

A capability cannot advance because a document labels it ready. It must satisfy the evidence required for the target state.

At minimum:

- source findings are traceable;
- baseline and treatment conditions are comparable where an intervention is claimed;
- supported and unsupported claims are separated;
- languages, systems, journeys, and contexts are explicit;
- evaluator fit and disagreement are visible;
- privacy, license, and representation risks are reviewed;
- operating requirements, failure handling, and ownership are known;
- costs, latency, safety, user-agency, and workflow regressions are considered.

Readiness applies only to the tested scope. Success in one journey cannot promote a universal language or product claim.

## Value Test

Count value only when the work changes a user, business, research, or governance outcome, such as:

- task completion or correctness;
- recovery, escalation, or time to resolution;
- trust, consent, safety, or user control;
- launch or market-expansion confidence;
- reduced rework or evaluator effort;
- a clearer product decision;
- a reusable treatment or evaluation pattern.

Fluent output, a higher aggregate score, or new infrastructure is not sufficient by itself.

## Integration Surface Ladder

Expose customer-facing surfaces only in response to proven delivery needs:

1. Reports and review packets.
2. Portable files, schemas, rubrics, and evidence summaries.
3. Local validation or analysis CLI.
4. SDK or notebook interfaces.
5. MCP tools for approved agent-native workflows.
6. CI regression checks.
7. REST API.
8. Hosted dashboard and managed runner.

Each step is a separate hypothesis. Interest in a report does not validate an API or hosted platform.

The current default is reports, review packets, and inspectable files. Later surfaces require repeated internal or customer pull plus a decision record.

## Productization Trigger

A component becomes product infrastructure only after repeated research or customer workflows prove that it should exist.

Evidence for productization should include:

- the same need appearing across multiple runs, studies, or engagements;
- a stable enough contract to support versioning;
- measurable reduction in effort, error, or delivery risk;
- an identified owner and maintenance cost;
- no simpler artifact or process that solves the need adequately.

## Engineering Boundary

Prefer a single clear research artifact over a generalized abstraction. Build an abstraction only when repeated use has made the common contract observable.
