# Applied Lab

The Applied Lab turns native-adoption diagnosis into targeted interventions and decision-grade implementation guidance.

It is the bridge between research findings and practical organizational value. It is not generic AI consulting and does not bypass research or product-readiness gates.

## First Service Shape

The first expected service is a Native Adoption Analysis.

A scoped engagement may:

- define one to three important agent workflows;
- establish language, locale, community, and representation boundaries;
- freeze expected behavior and evaluation criteria;
- run a baseline;
- identify cross-language capability gaps;
- diagnose the earliest lifecycle failure points;
- prioritize intervention candidates;
- test one intervention when access and scope permit;
- deliver evidence, limitations, decisions, and implementation guidance.

The exact offer, buyer, duration, and price remain hypotheses until commercial validation.

## Delivery Lifecycle

1. **Frame** - define the user, workflow, system, context, decision, risk, and success condition.
2. **Register** - version the cases, ground truth, baseline, metrics, evidence plan, and review requirements.
3. **Baseline** - observe the existing system and preserve run, trace, and evaluation evidence.
4. **Diagnose** - identify the earliest lifecycle break, affected dimension, uncertainty, and plausible mechanism.
5. **Treat** - implement the smallest intervention that could address the target failure.
6. **Retest** - compare matched conditions and check target outcomes plus regressions.
7. **Decide** - promote, revise, reject, or expand the intervention with explicit rationale.
8. **Package** - produce a report, handoff, method update, reusable pattern, or product implication.

Do not expand language, agent, workflow, or platform coverage until one narrow loop can pass through all eight stages.

## Intervention Principle

Test the smallest intervention tied to the diagnosed failure first.

Examples include:

- prompt or instruction structure;
- tool descriptions or schemas;
- locale-specific knowledge representation;
- clarification policy;
- routing or escalation behavior;
- response-style constraints;
- retrieval organization;
- deterministic validation or repair.

The intervention must identify:

- target failure and lifecycle stage;
- expected mechanism;
- implementation version;
- matched baseline;
- success and regression measures;
- rollback or rejection condition;
- known scope and limitations.

Broad tuning without a diagnosed target does not count as an Applied Lab experiment.

## Handoff Shape

An applied handoff should be usable without overstating certainty. Depending on scope, it may include:

- executive decision memo;
- method and scope summary;
- baseline and retest comparison;
- failure map;
- evidence and review summary;
- representation and limitation statement;
- intervention specification or treatment backlog;
- implementation artifacts;
- regression checks;
- supported and unsupported claims;
- next decision and owner.

Raw sensitive evidence, evaluator identities, credentials, and customer-specific proprietary material are not exposed by default.

## Value And Reuse

An Applied Lab engagement should identify the decision it changed and the outcome expected to improve.

Reusable patterns may be promoted only when they remain useful across more than one adjacent case, workflow, or system. Reuse attempts and failures should be recorded rather than hidden.

Repeated handoff needs may justify product engineering under the maturity and
productization rules in [`PRODUCTIZATION.md`](PRODUCTIZATION.md).

## Research Independence

Customer access and funding may improve the evidence base, but customers do not purchase favorable findings. Reports must preserve contradictory evidence, limitations, invalid runs, and claim boundaries.
