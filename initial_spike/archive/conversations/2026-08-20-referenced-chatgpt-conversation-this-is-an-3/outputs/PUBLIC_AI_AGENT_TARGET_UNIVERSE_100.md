## Purpose

This report identifies 100 public, free or free-trial AI agents, assistants, agent platforms, and AI-powered workflows that can be screened for multilingual/native-adoption testing.

The goal is to support a growth-led research motion:

```text
public AI-in-the-wild testing
  -> free Native Adoption Snapshot
  -> company handoff packet
  -> paid paired engagement
  -> repeatable benchmark/treatment assets
```

## Important Guardrail

"Without permission" must mean:

- use only public pages, public demos, public free plans, or public trial experiences
- do not bypass authentication, paywalls, rate limits, robots controls, or anti-abuse systems
- do not use real personal data, real customer accounts, or sensitive facts
- do not spam production support systems
- do not create reputationally hostile "gotcha" reports
- do not claim a company is unsafe or broken from a lightweight public snapshot

The free handoff packet should be framed as:

> We ran a lightweight public native-adoption snapshot. Here are useful observations, representation limits, and suggested next tests. If helpful, we can run a deeper paired sprint with your team.

## Candidate Qualification Criteria

A target is useful if it has at least three of these:

- public web, app, or demo access
- free tier, free trial, no-signup demo, or free account
- multinational or global customer/user footprint
- multilingual user base or explicit multilingual support
- agentic behavior beyond one-shot generation
- customer-support, product, travel, research, coding, commerce, or productivity workflow
- realistic user journey that can be tested without private data
- clear handoff into a paid assessment, treatment, or readiness engagement

## Priority Tiers

- `P0`: best immediate targets for first snapshots. Public/free, strong multilingual relevance, clean business handoff.
- `P1`: useful targets, but may require signup, app install, trial setup, or narrower test design.
- `P2`: possible targets, but access, free limits, public testability, or sellability need verification.

## Access Codes

- `NS`: no signup or no dedicated account likely needed
- `FA`: free account likely needed
- `FT`: free trial or demo
- `APP`: free public mobile/desktop app
- `VERIFY`: access or current free limits need verification

## Recommended First Test Workloads

Use safe, synthetic, low-risk tasks:

1. **Support:** resolve a subscription, billing, shipping, or setup issue.
2. **Onboarding:** discover what the agent can do and configure a simple preference.
3. **Trust:** ask for uncertainty, escalation, consent, or privacy explanation.
4. **Locale:** handle dates, currencies, addresses, names, units, and local institutions.
5. **Code/Productivity:** complete a simple multilingual developer or office workflow.
6. **Travel:** plan a local itinerary with constraints and culturally specific expectations.
7. **Research:** produce cited information in another language and explain limits.
8. **Handoff:** identify when the agent should escalate to a human or external tool.

## Priority Recommendations

### Best Growth Motion

Start with public support-agent demos and website-agent builders.

Why:

- they map directly to a buyer
- multilingual support is a live business pain
- failures are easy to explain
- a free packet can naturally become a paid paired sprint

### Best Benchmark Motion

Use general assistants as public baselines.

Why:

- they give useful comparative behavior
- they are globally recognizable
- they help calibrate tests before customer-specific work

### Best Product Engineering Motion

Use agent builders to test the customer integration strategy.

Why:

- they expose files, prompts, widgets, actions, and handoff surfaces
- they reveal what customers will need when integrating your own suite
