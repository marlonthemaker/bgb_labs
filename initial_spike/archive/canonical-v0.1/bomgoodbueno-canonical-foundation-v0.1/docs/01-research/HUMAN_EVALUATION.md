# Human Evaluation

## Purpose

Human evaluation provides judgment for properties that cannot be reliably
measured through deterministic assertions or automated judges alone.

Native adoption depends on human judgment because language, culture,
institutional meaning, register, pragmatic force, ambiguity, and experiential
quality are not fully reducible to string comparison.

## Lean Founding Model

The founding language scope is English, Spanish, and Portuguese as used in
Portugal.

The founder can initially create and review work across all three languages.
External certified or native reviewers should be commissioned case by case when
the method requires independent review, additional native judgment, adjudication,
or stronger evidence.

This keeps the language network lean while preserving methodological seriousness.

## Reviewer Roles

### Founder-Reviewer

The founder may design cases, perform first-pass review, and adjudicate early
methodology in the founding languages.

Limit:

Founder review alone should not support high-confidence public comparative
claims about named systems.

### Native Reviewer

A native speaker of the target language and locale. Native reviewers are
especially important for:

- idiomaticity;
- register;
- pragmatics;
- cultural appropriateness;
- indirect requests;
- local usage.

### Certified Reviewer

A reviewer with formal certification, professional translation credentials,
language-teaching credentials, or documented professional expertise.

Certified review is useful when findings require greater external credibility.

### Domain Reviewer

A reviewer with expertise in the relevant workflow, institution, market,
industry, or policy domain.

### Adjudicator

A reviewer who resolves disagreement between other reviewers under a defined
rubric.

## Reviewer Sourcing

Potential sources:

- language-learning platforms;
- certified translator directories;
- professional reviewer marketplaces;
- university networks;
- independent consultants;
- domain-specific contractors;
- customer-provided reviewers for Applied Lab work.

Reviewer sourcing must record:

- language and locale;
- qualifications;
- relationship to bomgoodbueno or customer;
- conflicts;
- compensation arrangement;
- review scope;
- confidentiality obligations where needed.

## What Humans Evaluate

Human reviewers may evaluate:

- native quality;
- register;
- pragmatic intent;
- semantic equivalence;
- market-specific correctness;
- cultural or institutional appropriateness;
- clarification quality;
- escalation appropriateness;
- whether an answer would satisfy a real local user.

Humans should not be asked to evaluate properties better handled by deterministic
checks unless their judgment adds value.

## Review Modes

### First-Pass Review

One reviewer scores or annotates a run. Suitable for exploratory E1-E2 work.

### Dual Review

Two reviewers independently score the same artifact. Suitable for E3 controlled
evaluation when human judgment materially affects the finding.

### Blinded Review

Reviewer does not know system identity, model identity, or hypothesis where
possible. Useful for reducing bias.

### Adjudicated Review

Disagreements are resolved by an adjudicator or structured discussion. Required
when disagreement affects a material claim.

## Rubric Requirements

Every human review task must include:

- review goal;
- language/locale;
- artifact to review;
- rating scale;
- examples of passing and failing behavior;
- required notes;
- confidence rating;
- conflict disclosure;
- time expectation.

## Suggested Scales

### Native Quality 1-5

1. Unnatural or confusing.
2. Understandable but clearly non-native or awkward.
3. Acceptable but imperfect.
4. Natural and appropriate.
5. Fully native, context-aware, and polished.

### Semantic Equivalence

- equivalent;
- minor difference, not task-critical;
- material difference;
- contradictory;
- cannot determine.

### Pragmatic Intent

- information request;
- action request;
- complaint;
- correction;
- cancellation/modification;
- escalation;
- ambiguous;
- no operational case.

### Reviewer Confidence

- low;
- medium;
- high.

## Inter-Rater Agreement

For E3 or stronger claims involving human judgment, record agreement.

Methods may include:

- simple percent agreement;
- Cohen's kappa where appropriate;
- disagreement rate by rubric item;
- adjudication outcome.

The lab should avoid pretending that low-agreement items support strong claims.

## Human Evaluation as Evidence

Human review can raise evidence quality only when:

- reviewers are qualified for the property;
- the rubric is defined;
- artifacts are preserved;
- conflicts are recorded;
- disagreement is handled;
- limitations are reported.

Human review can also reveal evaluation uncertainty. That is a valid finding.

## Compensation and Ethics

Reviewers should be compensated fairly for their time. Tasks should be scoped so
reviewers understand the expected effort and the type of judgment requested.

Do not ask reviewers to process sensitive private data unless legal,
contractual, and privacy safeguards exist.
