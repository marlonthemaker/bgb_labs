# Mission, Vision, and Principles

## Mission

bomgoodbueno advances the democratization of digital intelligence through
applied native adoption analysis.

The lab studies whether agentic systems can reliably understand, reason, act,
and communicate within the languages, cultures, institutions, workflows, and
markets they claim to support.

## Vision

People should not receive weaker digital intelligence because they operate in a
language, market, institution, or workflow that was not the default design
environment of the system.

The long-term vision is an AI ecosystem where capability claims are observable,
testable, comparable, and accountable across languages and local contexts.

## Foundational Thesis

Digital intelligence is not meaningfully democratized merely because a model can
generate text in someone's language. It is democratized when people can access
equivalent capabilities through their own language, cultural context,
institutions, workflows, and modes of interaction.

## Foundational Principles

### 1. Access ≠ Adoption

Availability of an AI product in a country, language, or interface does not mean
the system has been adopted natively by that context.

Access answers:

> Can a user reach the system?

Adoption asks:

> Can the user accomplish equivalent work through the system in their own
> context?

### 2. Support ≠ Parity

A vendor claim that a system "supports Portuguese" or "supports Spanish" is not
equivalent to a claim that Portuguese or Spanish users receive the same
capability, safety, reliability, or workflow correctness as English users.

Support can be binary. Parity is empirical.

### 3. Native Capability Is Not Translated Output

Native capability is the ability to understand and perform work inside a local
context. Translation may be one ingredient, but translated output alone does not
prove semantic parity, task parity, operational parity, institutional
nativeness, or experiential nativeness.

### 4. Capability Is Behavioral

Capability should be measured by what the system does, not merely by what the
system says it can do.

Evaluation should inspect:

- interpretation;
- decomposition;
- reasoning;
- retrieval;
- tool choice;
- tool arguments;
- policy adherence;
- response quality;
- outcome correctness;
- trace evidence.

### 5. Claims Require Provenance

Every substantive claim should identify its source, evidence class, scope,
limitations, and date.

Unsupported claims must be labeled as hypotheses or observations, not findings.

### 6. Public-System Testing Must Be Responsible

AILITW observes systems in the wild, but it must not exploit, overload,
deceive, harass, or bypass public systems.

Responsible public-system testing requires:

- minimal interaction;
- no credential theft or access circumvention;
- no attempt to extract private data;
- no operational disruption;
- no publication of details that create avoidable harm;
- clear distinction between public observation and permissioned testing.

### 7. Humans Remain Part of the Measurement System

Native adoption cannot be measured only through automatic scores. Some
properties require native or certified human review, including pragmatics,
register, idiomaticity, cultural appropriateness, ambiguous speech acts, and
local institutional meaning.

Human evaluation must be structured, auditable, compensated fairly, and treated
as evidence with known reliability limits.

### 8. Evaluation Reliability Is a First-Class Problem

AI judges, human reviewers, translated benchmarks, and deterministic assertions
all have failure modes. The lab should not hide these weaknesses.

Evaluation design must specify:

- what property is being measured;
- why the chosen evaluator can measure it;
- how disagreement is handled;
- what confidence level the evidence supports;
- when a result should be considered inconclusive.

### 9. Workflow Correctness Matters

Agentic systems must be evaluated as systems that act, not only as systems that
answer.

Workflow correctness includes:

- selecting the right task;
- preserving task-critical information;
- choosing the right tool;
- passing correct tool arguments;
- respecting policy and authority;
- handling dependencies;
- escalating when necessary;
- representing uncertainty.

### 10. Scientific Independence

bomgoodbueno may do paid applied work, but research claims must remain separable
from commercial incentives. Applied Lab results may inform methodology, but
public research must identify conflicts, scope, limitations, and evidence.

## Founding Languages

The founding languages are:

- English (`en`);
- Spanish (`es`);
- Portuguese as used in Portugal (`pt-PT`).

This triad allows the lab to begin with languages the founder can personally
review while still testing cross-language capability parity across distinct
linguistic, cultural, and market contexts.

## Commitments

bomgoodbueno commits to:

- making precise claims;
- preserving methodological humility;
- separating support from parity;
- distinguishing access from adoption;
- making evidence inspectable;
- designing for local users, not abstract global averages;
- building product infrastructure from research needs;
- producing public goods when doing so is safe and useful.

## Unresolved Questions

- What level of evidence should be required before publishing named system
  comparisons?
- When should a system owner be notified before publication?
- Which parts of AILITW should remain observational, and which require explicit
  permission?
- How should the lab handle languages outside the founder's personal competence?
- What public-good licensing strategy best supports adoption without weakening
  commercial sustainability?
