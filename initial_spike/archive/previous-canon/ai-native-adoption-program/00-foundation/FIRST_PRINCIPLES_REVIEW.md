# First-Principles Review

ID: `DOC-FOUND-0007`
Version: v0.2
Status: proposed

## Purpose

This document scrutinizes the AI Native Adoption Program from first principles and defines the quality bar for building a world-class research, product, and governance system for accessible AI across languages and cultures.

## Core Premise

The program should not exist to prove that multilingual AI has problems. That is already obvious to many users.

The program should exist to produce decision-grade knowledge and practical capability that help organizations make agentic AI genuinely usable, trustworthy, and valuable in more languages, locales, and cultural contexts.

## First Principles

### 1. Native Adoption Is A System Outcome

Native adoption is not equal to translation quality, BLEU score, fluency, or model benchmark rank. It emerges from the interaction of:

- model behavior
- product surfaces
- language and locale conventions
- tool and permission flows
- trust signals
- user goals
- cultural expectations
- accessibility constraints
- evaluator assumptions
- organizational risk tolerance

Implication: benchmark items must be journeys, not isolated prompts.

### 2. A Language Is Not A Market, And A Locale Is Not A Culture

Language tags and locale data are necessary but insufficient. `pt-PT`, `pt-BR`, and Portuguese-speaking communities in migration, education, customer support, or medicine may require different assumptions.

Implication: every study needs a declared language context and a humility statement about what it does not represent.

### 3. Evidence Must Be Useful For Decisions

The program should produce evidence that can change a roadmap, treatment, offer, or governance decision.

Implication: every major artifact should answer:

- What decision could this change?
- What evidence would reverse it?
- Who is affected if we are wrong?

### 4. Trust Is A Product Feature

For multilingual agents, trust is not a compliance wrapper. It is part of the user experience: uncertainty, consent, safety boundaries, escalation, and user control must feel natural in the user's language and context.

Implication: trust metrics must be part of adoption, not a separate afterthought.

### 5. Black-Box Adaptation Is Valuable Only If It Preserves Agency

External layers can improve usability, but they can also hide uncertainty, force cultural assumptions, or overcorrect user intent.

Implication: every treatment needs regression checks for task accuracy, safety, cost, latency, and user agency.

### 6. Community Validity Matters

A fluent evaluator is not automatically a representative user. A native speaker is not automatically qualified for every domain, region, class register, accessibility need, or cultural context.

Implication: evaluator metadata and disagreement are part of the evidence.

### 7. Product Claims Must Be Narrower Than Internal Findings

Internal findings can be tentative. Customer-facing claims must be traceable, scoped, reviewed, and limitation-aware.

Implication: product readiness gates must be stricter than research readiness gates.

## Current Strengths

- The Wild-Lab-Sandbox loop is a strong operating model.
- The program avoids reducing multilingual quality to translation.
- The ID system is a good foundation for traceability.
- Evidence, experiment, and human evaluation standards exist early.
- The v0.2 domains correctly elevate Intelligence Graph, Registries, Trust, and Product.
- The local-first platform bias is appropriate for early learning and cost discipline.

## Current Gaps

### Strategic Gaps

- Value-generation logic is implied but not explicit.
- Buyer, user, and affected-community distinctions are underdeveloped.
- The repo needs clearer differentiation between research asset, product capability, and offer.

### Research Gaps

- Locale and community validity need a dedicated standard.
- Accessibility is mentioned indirectly but not operationalized.
- The program needs clearer stop conditions for weak signals and non-transferable findings.

### Governance Gaps

- Risk management is present but not yet mapped to an AI governance operating model.
- Management-system controls are not yet explicit.
- Trust review needs defined authority before customer-facing use.

### Platform Gaps

- Registries are specified conceptually, but no first schemas exist.
- The first traceability slice is not implemented.
- No minimal artifact directory structure exists yet for runs and evidence.

## Optimization Priorities

1. Add explicit standards alignment.
2. Add locale, community, and accessibility validity rules.
3. Add value-generation model.
4. Strengthen risk register with market, governance, accessibility, and community harms.
5. Expand operating model roles for Trust, Product, and Community/Locale Review.
6. Turn the first closed loop into structured schemas and a traceability slice.

## Quality Bar

The program is world-class only if it can repeatedly produce:

- a realistic multilingual journey
- a documented failure
- evidence with chain of custody
- a plausible mechanism
- a tested treatment
- matched baseline/treatment comparison
- a finding with limits
- a decision
- a product implication
- a claim boundary

## World-Class Test

For any proposed finding or product claim, a skeptical expert should be able to ask:

1. What exactly was tested?
2. Who was represented?
3. Who was not represented?
4. What evidence supports it?
5. What evidence contradicts it?
6. What changed because of it?
7. What could go wrong if this is deployed?
8. What claim is safe to make?
9. What claim must not be made?
10. What would we do differently for the next language, locale, or community?
