# Principles

ID: `DOC-FOUND-0003`
Version: v0.1

## Research Principles

- Measure adoption, not just output quality.
- Treat language, culture, product context, and agent behavior as coupled variables.
- Prefer reproducible evidence over persuasive anecdotes.
- Separate observations from explanations and explanations from interventions.
- Record uncertainty explicitly.
- State who and what each study does not represent.
- Prefer decision-grade learning over broad but shallow coverage.

## Product Principles

- Build tools that help researchers, evaluators, and builders close the learning loop.
- Make evidence traceable from raw observation to finding.
- Keep black-box adaptation useful even when model providers do not cooperate.
- Optimize for small complete loops before broad benchmark coverage.
- Make product claims narrower than internal findings.
- Count value only when user, business, trust, access, or operational outcomes improve.

## Evaluation Principles

- Evaluate user journeys, not isolated prompts only.
- Include human review where automated metrics cannot judge native comprehension, trust, cultural fit, or task appropriateness.
- Compare against matched baselines.
- Retest treatments in the same conditions where failures were found.
- Treat accessibility as part of native adoption, not a separate polish step.
- Preserve evaluator disagreement when it reveals context, ambiguity, or risk.

## Ethical Principles

- Do not treat speakers of lower-resource languages as edge cases.
- Avoid extracting cultural judgments without appropriate context.
- Label limits of evaluator expertise.
- Protect user data, especially multilingual free-form text that may contain sensitive identity information.
- Avoid claiming community representation from convenience samples.
- Treat affected communities as stakeholders, not only markets.
